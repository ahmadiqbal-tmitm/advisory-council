Agent #2 · Built in public

AI Advisory Council

Four advisors inside Claude, each a master of one field. Add one link, then bring them your next decision.

Takes about two minutes. No code. No account to create.

Who is on the council

Socrates · questioning
Tests your definitions, assumptions and contradictions. He opens every council session with one question.
Sun Tzu · strategy
Positioning, timing, strengths and weaknesses.
Marshall McLuhan · technology and media
Whether a product idea has legs, and how to think in a world of AI and agents.
Marcus Aurelius · way of life
Temperament, setbacks, other people, and what is within your control.

What you need

  • A Claude account on a plan that includes custom connectors. Check claude.com/pricing.
  • About two minutes.

Step 1: Add the council to Claude (1 minute)

  1. In Claude, open Settings, then Connectors.
  2. Click Add custom connector.
  3. Name it AI Advisory Council and paste the address below.
Connector address
https://advisorycouncil.ahmads.build/mcp

Why this matters: a connector is a plug that lets Claude reach the advisors' material. Without it, Claude is only guessing at what they would say.

Step 2: Ask the council (1 minute)

Start a new chat, check the connector is switched on, and paste this with your own decision:

Prompt
Ask the council whether I should ___.

Here is what happens:

  1. Socrates asks you one question to find out what you are really deciding.
  2. After your answer he writes his understanding in five short lines, and suggests which advisors to bring in.
  3. Each advisor answers in their own words, briefly, and usually ends with a question for you.
  4. You see where they agree, where they differ, and which way the council leans.

Reply to any advisor to keep talking with them. In a hurry? Say "skip to the council" to go past Socrates.

Step 3: Or ask one advisor

Name the advisor and you get them alone, with no opening from Socrates:

Prompt
Ask Sun Tzu about this: ___

How it works

  • The council is a library, not a brain. It hands your Claude each advisor's material: their principles, how they speak, and for Socrates and Marcus Aurelius a bank of questions. Your own Claude writes the replies from it.
  • Nothing is stored. There are no accounts. The council keeps no record of what you ask.
  • It is free.
  • It offers perspective, not professional advice. On legal, financial or medical questions, it will point you to a real professional.

See how it is built

The code, the advisors' material and the build log are public on GitHub. Adding an advisor of your own takes one document and one entry in a list.

Why I built this

I had three real decisions in front of me: whether to join a partnership, how to start a workshop business, and whether to invest in a friend's company.

I wanted more than one opinion. So I built a council: four thinkers I keep going back to, each asked to look at the same question from their own field.

I built it for my own decisions first. Now anyone can add it to Claude.

What I learned

  • The prompts are the product. When the first replies came back as walls of text, I fixed it by rewriting three plain-text files. The code never changed.
  • Advisors should speak, not be described. "Sun Tzu: you are choosing between…" reads like a conversation. "Sun Tzu looks at the ground…" reads like a report.
  • Short wins. One question from Socrates gets you to value faster than a paragraph explaining what is about to happen.
  • Keep the server simple. It does no thinking and stores nothing, so it costs almost nothing to run.
